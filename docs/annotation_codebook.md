# Annotation guide

This guide defines manual labels for the project.

## Unit

The main unit is the paragraph.

Annotators should ask whether the paragraph develops one idea or behaves like an inventory inside prose.

## Main labels

### 0. No enumeration

The paragraph develops one idea without stacking many examples, categories, effects, qualities, or questions.

### 1. Mild enumeration

The paragraph has one limited list-like movement, but the inventory does not dominate the paragraph.

### 2. Heavy enumeration

The paragraph reads like an inventory. It keeps adding examples, categories, effects, qualities, domains, actors, or questions instead of developing one line of thought.

## Form labels

Use these labels when relevant.

comma_chain

A sentence uses repeated commas to pack several items into one sequence.

example_trigger

The paragraph uses phrases such as including, such as, for example, across, or ranging from.

question_chain

The paragraph turns a list into repeated questions or alternatives.

contrast_pair_chain

The paragraph uses repeated pairings such as not only this but also that, either this or that, or from this to that.

abstraction_cluster

The paragraph stacks abstract nouns such as readability, authorship, trust, legitimacy, transparency, accountability, or governance.

visible_list

The paragraph or surrounding text uses bullets, numbers, line breaks, or obvious list formatting.

## Rule

When unsure between two labels, choose the lower severity label unless the paragraph clearly feels list-like to an ordinary reader.
